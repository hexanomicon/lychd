# :material-script-text-outline: Incantation

Begin by saying what you want to bring into being. Until an intention has a form, another
person cannot inspect it, challenge it, or help it change. Giving it a boundary makes those
encounters possible: something belongs to this undertaking, and something remains outside it,
able to answer.

The Work follows this first distinction back to unmarked stillness. Before a mark, there is no
local perspective, deed, or outside from which an answer could arrive. Stillness trembles; the
native liturgy borrows **Spanda** for that first pulse. The pulse becomes a **Word** when Logos
gives the difference a name. **Brahmā** gives this beginning of form its image. As the mark
acquires an edge, a Circle opens in which something can take shape.

> **Without a boundary there is potential, but no one for whom the potential appears.**

This is **Nigredo**, the Word's first embodiment. An intention accepts the limits that let it
become a particular thing. It can now be inspected, contradicted, and repaired. Possibility
has found an address, and the next encounter can reveal what this first form failed to include.

## Let the Word incur a consequence

In the craft, this first articulation is **xDDD—eXtreme Documentation Driven Development**.
Establish Logos in documentation and specification before deriving implementation: the truth,
vocabulary, and boundaries that make the work intelligible. Ask what the form should exist to
do, then which seams make that purpose answerable.

That encounter needs more than a declaration. The **Verse** says what is intended; an accepted
**Covenant** gives the intention architectural law. **Matter** answers with what was actually
made: source, focused tests, lockfiles, and maintained receipts. Compare that evidence with the
Word rather than treating the declaration as its own fulfillment. **[State of Work](../../state-of-the-work.md)**
records the crossing into manifestation, so the Vessel can be judged against the purpose and
limits that shaped it.

The first form can now be held to account, but a continuing self requires further encounters.
It needs the fire that makes action possible, the offices through which it receives and judges,
and a history whose consequences can return. The Lich chapter's [First Invocation](../../sepulcher/lich/index.md#the-first-invocation)
introduces those names together; the [Lexicon](../../lexicon/index.md) keeps their meanings.

## The logic behind the next task {#logic-behind-logic}

The Magus solves one problem, builds its workflow, and begins the same labor for the next.
Each unfamiliar project asks again for decomposition, evidence, criticism, and a way to know
when the work is done. More Agents can multiply the labor while leaving its organization in
one person's hands. The Magus becomes the bottleneck: every answer returns with another demand
to decide what should happen next.

The deeper ambition is a craft that learns how to approach another craft. An earlier decision
may have turned on a useful distinction. A Lich should recognize that relation in a different
task, carry it forward, and discover where the new situation defeats it. This requires learning
to frame a problem, seek missing evidence, choose a method, and revise the method through
consequence.

**The generality sought by the Work reaches into the making of workflows themselves.** A learned
answer helps when the question is familiar. A learned way of investigating can discover which
question the unfamiliar work requires.

**[Viveka begins before the cut](../../sepulcher/lich/blade.md#measure-before-the-cut):**
examine the direction before committing to it. Where thought cannot settle the choice, a small
experiment should return the observation that can change the course.

This is the Work's ambition for **AGI**: a Magus entrusts unfamiliar work without having to
supply every next intellectual move. The human can then originate purposes and live beyond the
Circle. Those purposes and their authority still have an owner; [Blade](../../sepulcher/lich/blade.md#the-magus-bottleneck)
must increasingly make the judgment its own. [Illumination](illumination.md#learning-the-cut)
follows how a correction can become that capacity in the next encounter.

A flawed Word can still summon a flawed body. If authority is left ambiguous, the body cannot
distinguish what it can do from what it may do. If identity remains vague, an act can lose the
one who must answer for it. If memory has no boundary—no way to question, correct, or release
what returns—an old account can hold every later encounter captive. Nigredo asks the Magus to
make the first world small enough to answer for, and its first word clear enough that another
voice can show where it is wrong.

The edge is drawn. Now an [Invocation](invocation.md) can cross it.
