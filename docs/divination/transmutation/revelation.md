# :material-eye-outline: Revelation

What did the encounter disclose?

Revelation is the disclosure of findings through accountable interpretation. Observed results
meet the criteria already declared in [Canon](canon.md), with attributable evidence kept close
to each conclusion. Failures remain visible, as do gaps in capture that limit what can be
known. [Numbers / Numeri](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/0), whose title derives from
its censuses, offers a quiet echo of taking account across a journey.

In the field-note experiment, the observed result is a lost observation. An insufficient
conflict rule may explain the loss, but that diagnosis remains a hypothesis until the evidence
establishes it. Retaining both versions is a proposed correction. Measurement records what
occurred, diagnosis seeks to explain why, and a proposal describes what might improve it.
A score alone cannot prove a cause. The proposed correction needs another trial, and adoption
remains subject to the responsible owner's decision.

[Drift's returning findings](../../sepulcher/extensions/drift/returning-findings.md) keeps
these distinctions through uncertainty, attribution, and bounded correction requests.
Drift reports evidence; the receiving owner decides what may change. A finding does not
itself rewrite the workflow or promote a candidate.
