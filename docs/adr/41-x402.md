# :material-cash-sync: 41. Economic Effects: The Toll

!!! abstract "Context and Problem Statement"
    Local compute, remote Portals, and peer labor carry resource or monetary cost. LychD needs to
    account for those costs and may eventually settle a priced remote request, but a quote is
    controlled by another party and money movement is difficult to reverse. A response code,
    model, middleware hook, or Dispatcher choice must never become spending authority.

## Requirements

- **Challenge is proposal:** HTTP `402`, an invoice, or any provider quote may describe terms; it
  cannot authorize signing, settlement, retry, identity, access, or a larger budget.
- **Separated planes:** Quote parsing and accounting hold no spend key. Signing and settlement sit
  behind a narrow effect boundary with no model or ordinary tool surface.
- **Pinned commitment:** Authorization binds principal and budget owner, merchant or service,
  method and canonical destination, request digest, purchased resource, asset and network,
  maximum amount including fees, expiry, redirect policy, and idempotency key.
- **Worst-case reservation:** Accounting reserves the full authorized exposure before a signer is
  invoked and consumes it atomically.
- **Explicit consent:** Novel or significant spend requires live Magus consent. Only a declared,
  bounded low-risk class may use preauthorization.
- **Durable reconciliation:** Quote, reservation, authorization, submission, settlement, delivery,
  refund, expiry, and ambiguous outcome survive crashes and restores as an idempotent receipt
  chain.
- **Key confinement:** Private keys, signatures, bearer proofs, preimages, invoices, and wallet
  credentials never enter prompts, configuration values meant for ordinary projection, logs,
  traces, or artifacts.
- **Protocol separation:** x402, L402, Lightning invoices, Wallet Connect, Nostr Zaps, and future
  rails are distinct adapters, not aliases.
- **Currency-neutral accounting:** Tithe quotas for tokens, images, concurrency, queue weight, and
  hardware time remain useful with every payment adapter disabled.

## Considered Options

!!! failure "Option 1: Automatic 402 middleware"
    A generic interceptor receives a challenge, pays it from a system wallet, and retries the
    request.

    This lets an untrusted server command the purse and obscures merchant substitution, quote
    drift, redirects, loops, concurrent requests, paid-but-undelivered work, and uncertain
    settlement.

!!! failure "Option 2: Agents or Dispatcher hold a wallet tool"
    A reasoning component compares prices and directly signs the selected transaction.

    Planning evidence is not authority. Prompt injection, schema confusion, or a routing error
    would cross directly into irreversible value movement.

!!! failure "Option 3: Internal quotas only"
    Local counters can protect resources but cannot describe an external quote, prove bounded
    consent, settle a remote service, or reconcile the result. They are necessary Tithe
    accounting, not the whole Toll.

!!! success "Option 4: Counting House plus Irreversible Gate"
    Toll separates price discovery and reservation from one independently verified settlement
    effect. Protocol and rail adapters surround one host-owned commitment and reconciliation law.

## Decision Outcome

**Toll is adopted as an optional economic Domain manifested through governed accounting
middleware and isolated effect handlers.** It is not a mandatory wallet service, a global
auto-payment interceptor, or authority delegated to an Agent.

### 1. The Counting House

The Counting House may parse a versioned challenge, normalize integer monetary units, compare
candidate costs, reserve a bounded budget, and reconcile receipts. It holds no spend key and
cannot settle.

The Dispatcher may consider admitted quotes only after capability, privacy, policy, and resource
feasibility. A cheaper route is not automatically eligible. A payment may purchase a precisely
defined quota grant, but cannot mint a Sigil, widen a Ward scope, expose memory, reveal a tool,
bypass consent, or displace protected local work.

### 2. The Irreversible Gate

The isolated signer independently verifies the pinned authorization and current reservation,
enforces per-effect and rolling caps, atomically consumes the reservation, and permits at most the
one settlement operation named by that authorization. Unknown versions, schemes, networks,
destinations, amounts, fee bounds, or changed quotes fail closed.

The Magus may administer policy but is not exempt from wallet safety. Approval means _these exact
maximum terms_, never “whatever the next challenge asks.”

### 3. The Paid-Request Rite

1. A destination-pinned connector produces a candidate request and asks for a quote.
2. The Counting House validates merchant, request digest, resource, amount, fees, asset, network,
   expiry, redirect policy, and budget.
3. Policy rejects it, applies one matching bounded standing authority, or parks the Run for live
   consent over the exact terms.
4. Accounting reserves the worst-case total.
5. The signer revalidates the same facts, signs once, submits once, and records the external-effect
   identity.
6. After independently authorized settlement, the original connector may make at most one
   proof-bearing replay to the same pinned destination. It cannot initiate another payment.
7. The ledger reconciles settlement and useful delivery. Uncertain settlement is investigated,
   never answered by signing again.

Payment and delivery are not atomic. A settled payment may yield no useful result; a successful
response does not make content true. Refund, credit, dispute, expiry, paid-but-undelivered, and
ambiguous outcomes remain visible.

### 4. Tithe, Protocols, and Extensions

Tithe accounting binds resource use to a stable principal or service grant and works without
money. Owned Legion delegation may be currency-free, but still requires quotas, reservation,
attribution, and evidence.

The first payment implementation must pin one exact protocol profile and conformance corpus.
Extensions may contribute bounded quote parsers, settlement rails, signers, and paid Portal
connectors. They may not install arbitrary global spending middleware, hold unrestricted keys,
define a second ledger truth, or bypass host authorization.

### 5. Receipts Outlive Traces

Money does not rewind with a Phylactery restore. Spending remains closed after restore until the
receipt chain is reconciled against the external rail. Native
**[Oculus (29)](29-observability.md)** may observe redacted transitions and reconciliation lag,
but a trace is not the financial ledger.

## Delivery Boundary

This ADR accepts architecture, not payment capability. LychD currently has no Toll package, quote
or budget ledger, signer, settlement path, paid-request retry, or safe payment-challenge response.
**[State of the Work](../state-of-the-work.md#x402-payments)** owns that public boundary; the
**[Toll page](../sepulcher/extensions/toll.md)** owns user-facing doctrine.

The safe build order is: immutable financial identities and integer money types; quote, budget,
reservation, and receipt accounting without money movement; an isolated signer with hard caps;
one allowlisted test connector proven through crash and replay; then seller routes, metering, and
optional settlement adapters.

## Consequences

!!! success "Positive"
    - Economic consequence becomes attributable, bounded, interruptible, and reconcilable.
    - Local quotas remain independent of any cryptocurrency or payment network.
    - Protocol adapters can evolve without granting them wallet authority.

!!! failure "Negative"
    - Correct settlement requires durable accounting, reconciliation, and operational key custody.
    - Payment cannot be a transparent generic retry; callers must handle parked and ambiguous
      outcomes.
    - Marketplace, refunds, disputes, legal obligations, and abuse controls remain later domains.
