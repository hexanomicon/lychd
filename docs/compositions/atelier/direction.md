# :material-palette-outline: Direction

A palette alone will not tell an illustrator where an exception belongs. Direction makes the Magus's taste usable across several assets: what the work should feel like, which visual rules support that intention, and how a candidate will be judged.

## A reviewable visual system

`atelier.establish_style_bible@1` records the chosen composition, shape, proportion, palette, material, light, camera, typography, motion, and exclusions where they apply. Observations about references can inform these choices. They do not instruct the maker to copy protected expression or imitate a named living artist.

Make the reasoning visible in four layers. Begin with the **intent** and the response it seeks. Keep attributable **observations** about references separate from the Magus's **decisions**, including controlled exceptions. Then state concrete **tests** that will show whether a candidate follows those decisions.

Provider prompts, seeds, controls, and exploratory images can help discover this visual system. The accepted direction remains their governing reference, and each accepted asset records the exact direction revision used in its review.

## Review and correction

When a candidate fails, identify the relevant rule and decide whether the rule or its execution needs repair. `atelier.revise_from_correction@1` admits a bounded forward correction to that layer. Conflicting rules, an impossible target, or exhausted correction return exact non-completion.

A display can also change the judgment. `atelier.presenter_calibration@1` may compare how a viewer or presentation changes perception. Use that evidence to inform a direction revision; it cannot edit an accepted asset or certify every display.

The resulting visual language guides [Assets](assets.md) and [Motion](motion.md). Acting on it still requires separate admission for generation, spending, egress, publication, or a changed brief.
