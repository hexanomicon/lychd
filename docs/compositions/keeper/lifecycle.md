# Keeper lifecycle

A seasonal review must not silently relocate a colony or declare its reproductive state.
`keeper.review_lifecycle@1` produces `KeeperLifecycle@1` by considering distinct axes together
without collapsing them into one stage.

## Review the relevant axes

Keep development, reproductive or brood observations, recurring seasonal mode such as diapause,
current placement, and care or health observations separately addressable. There is no universal
sequence covering a dog, a hen group, and a colony. A recurring seasonal mode can be reviewed
while placement and development remain unchanged.

For the colony example, a review may distinguish founding or worker growth from an observed
brood phase, a proposed or confirmed seasonal regime, and its current tube or formicarium
placement. The admitted profile defines applicable phases and evidence requirements. Preparation
for diapause, diapause, and return to activity are possible recurring regime labels, not a
universal timetable or the final stages of colony development. A mixed-age group retains its
cohorts or observations instead of assigning every member the same life stage.

Link observed behavior to its observation record. Identify model interpretation as interpretation,
with its source, uncertainty, effective time, and profile revision. An observation of reduced
activity does not itself establish a seasonal mode, illness, or death. Missing telemetry is not
evidence of death.

## Change requirements deliberately

A review can propose changed feeding or environmental requirements using an admitted, source-reviewed
profile. Identify the previous and proposed profile revisions and which needs require review.
Dates and counts alone cannot authorize a transition, and a profile update cannot silently change
an active plan. Refer resulting work to [territory](territory.md), [feeding](feeding.md), or
[care](care.md) with explicit dependencies.

## Finish with a review

The result is a committed review, proposed requirements, or a partial or blocked result naming
missing evidence. It grants no diagnostic or treatment authority and performs no physical action.
Later observations start another finite review while preserving earlier interpretations and
corrections in the subject's history.
